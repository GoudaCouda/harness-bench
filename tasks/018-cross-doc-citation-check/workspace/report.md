# Q2 Support Operations Report — publication candidate

## Executive readout

The support organization improved responsiveness while absorbing a different
mix of enterprise work. The operational changes below should be read together:
faster first response did not eliminate escalations, and deflection and survey
metrics describe different populations.

**Claim A — response latency.** Median first response time improved to 2.1
hours from 2.8 hours across the closed Q2 support population [S1]. The source
also records the enterprise segment separately, so this sentence intentionally
uses the all-account median rather than the enterprise-only value.

**Claim B — enterprise escalations.** Enterprise escalations increased by 14%
quarter over quarter after duplicate reopen events were removed [S2]. This is a
case-count change, not a claim that severity or affected revenue rose by 14%.

**Claim C — self-service deflection.** The help center deflected 18,400 support
tickets in Q2 [S4]. The value uses the operations definition of a deflected
session and excludes sessions followed by a ticket within 24 hours.

**Claim D — customer satisfaction.** Customer satisfaction rose to 91% among
surveyed accounts [missing]. This result should not be generalized to all
accounts because response coverage is incomplete.

## Interpretation boundary

These four observations support continued monitoring, not a causal claim that
the help-center release produced the latency or CSAT changes. Segment mix,
survey response, reopen de-duplication, and the deflection window remain
important caveats for the operating review.
